# Grant Officer Metrics: Job Creation Track

To make a defensible decision, a Grant Officer needs metrics that balance **impact** (how many jobs), **need** (who gets them), **risk** (does the math hold up), and **resilience** (are we over-exposed?).

Here are the key metrics an agent should calculate and present for every applicant:

### 1. The Core Employment Metrics (The "Multiplier")
*   **Direct Jobs Estimated**: The estimated number of employees working directly for the applicant business.
*   **Indirect Jobs Supported**: The number of jobs sustained upstream at the applicant's suppliers, scaled by how much of their revenue comes from the applicant's industry.
*   **Total Job Footprint**: (Direct Jobs) + (Indirect Jobs). *This is the primary ranking metric.*
*   **Cost per Job Protected/Created**: (Grant Amount) / (Total Job Footprint). A classic economic development metric indicating ROI.

### 2. The Equity & Need Metrics
*   **Neighborhood Poverty Rate**: The poverty rate of the census tract where the applicant is located.
*   **Supply Chain Poverty Footprint**: A weighted average poverty rate of the census tracts where the *suppliers* are located. *Why this matters: A business in a wealthy tract might buy exclusively from suppliers in high-need tracts. Funding the wealthy business is actually a covert way to channel money into the high-need area.*

### 3. The Resilience & Diversification Metrics (Preventing the "Walmart Effect")
*   **Industry Concentration Risk (The "Eggs in One Basket" Score)**: What percentage of the corridor's total jobs are in the applicant's specific NAICS industry? If the grant heavily favors an industry that already dominates the corridor, the neighborhood becomes vulnerable to sector-specific shocks (e.g., funding another tech office when tech is already 60% of local jobs).
*   **Supply Chain Breadth (Node Count)**: How many *distinct* supplier businesses are impacted by the applicant's growth? Creating 50 jobs across 15 different suppliers is much more resilient than creating 50 jobs at a single massive supplier.
*   **Supplier Industry Variety**: How many *different NAICS industries* are represented in the applicant's supply chain? A diverse supply chain spreads the economic benefit across different sectors, making the local economy more robust.

### 4. The Risk & Context Metrics
*   **Cascade Depth (Hops)**: How many steps back into the supply chain does the impact reach? (e.g., "Reaches 3 hops deep into 12 distinct businesses").
*   **Fragility Flag (SBA Charge-offs)**: Does this supply chain rely on a business that has previously defaulted on an SBA loan? If yes, the supply chain is highly fragile, making the grant more critical to prevent collapse.
